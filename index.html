Parts, in plain English

What each part does and the one thing worth checking before you buy it.

Processor (CPU)

Runs the game logic and feeds the graphics card. Check: core count, the socket (AM5 or LGA 1700/1851) and, for gaming, extra cache like AMD’s X3D chips.

Graphics card (GPU)

Draws every frame and sets your resolution and frame rate. Check: video memory (8 GB is tight now, 16 GB is comfortable) and card length.

Motherboard

Connects every part and decides your upgrade options. Check: socket, DDR5 support, M.2 slots and a PCIe x16 slot for the GPU.

Memory (RAM)

Holds open games and apps. Check: 32 GB is the sweet spot, run two matched sticks and enable the EXPO or XMP profile.

Storage (SSD)

Holds your system and game library. Check: NVMe over SATA, and 1 to 2 TB so you aren’t deleting games every month.

Power supply (PSU)

Delivers clean power to every part. Check: wattage headroom, an 80+ Gold rating and a 12V-2x6 cable for modern high-end GPUs.

Cooling

Keeps the CPU from throttling. Check: socket support, cooler height against your case, and radiator size for liquid coolers.

Case

Sets airflow and what fits. Check: GPU length clearance, a mesh front and dust filters.
